# Social Impact

## Problem

Government schemes exist, but underserved citizens often struggle to
discover, understand, and navigate them. myScheme, the Government of
India's own unified scheme portal, lists thousands of central and state
schemes -- but browsing and filtering a portal in English is not the same
as knowing which of those schemes actually apply to *your* situation,
especially for someone with limited digital literacy, limited time, or
limited comfort reading formal bureaucratic language.

The gap isn't scheme availability. It's discoverability, explainability,
and language access.

## Solution

SchemeSaathi converts complex scheme information into personalized, simple
guidance. A person describes their situation in their own words -- in
English, Hindi, or a mix -- and gets back a short, honest explanation of
which schemes may fit, what's missing to be sure, what documents they'd
need, and how to apply, with the official government source attached to
every claim.

## Potential impact

- **Better scheme discoverability** -- turning a formal, ministry-by-
  ministry search process into a plain-language conversation.
- **Reduced information barriers** -- explaining eligibility criteria in
  everyday language instead of bureaucratic phrasing.
- **Better accessibility** -- a guided profile form and quick-example
  personas lower the bar for someone unsure how to phrase their situation.
- **Multilingual access** -- starting with English and Hindi, with an
  architecture that extends to more Indian languages without a redesign.
- **Better understanding of eligibility** -- showing what matched, what's
  missing, and what didn't match, rather than a flat yes/no.
- **Easier navigation** -- documents needed and application steps are
  surfaced alongside the recommendation, not left as a separate search.

We have not run a field pilot, so we do not claim measured outcomes here --
only the mechanism by which the tool is intended to help. Any impact
numbers would need to come from an actual deployment with real users, not
be assumed at prototype stage.
